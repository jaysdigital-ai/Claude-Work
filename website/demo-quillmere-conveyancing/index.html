What is conveyancing?

It’s the part of property law that moves a home from one name to another. It sounds simple. It rarely is.

Behind a single signature sit contract terms, mortgages, the type of title and tenancy, council regulations and zoning. Our job is to read all of it before you commit to any of it.

How it works 01 / 04

The contract

Before you sign anything, we go through it with you: the title details, what’s included and what isn’t, any special conditions, and how long you have between signing and completion. Pulling out later is rarely simple, so this is where the care goes.

Searches & inspections

Once your offer is accepted, the building inspection is arranged and we check with council and government authorities for anything that has an interest in the property. We also tell you whether it suits what you actually plan to do with it.

Finance & adjustments

We coordinate with your lender, arrange the stamp duty payment, deal with any outstanding debts and work out the adjustments for council rates, water and land tax, so each side pays exactly their share and not a day more.

Settlement day

Funds are handed over in exchange for the certificate of title and the keys. We attend so you don’t have to. Afterwards the change of ownership is registered and the relevant authorities are told. Then it’s yours.

B

Selling a property

Whether you’re deciding if it’s time, sprucing the place up for a better price or still looking for an agent, start with the contract.

The contract
Legal terms, inclusions, exclusions and any special requirements, plus what you’re obliged to disclose.
The deposit
Paid by the purchaser once the contract is binding, usually held in your agent’s trust account.
Before settlement
We coordinate with your bank and the purchaser’s conveyancer. You arrange to disconnect the power.
Settlement
Rates and land tax are apportioned, and the agent releases your deposit, less their commission.
